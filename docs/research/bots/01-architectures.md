# Architecture for a fast six-max no-limit Hold’em bot

**Recommendation.** Ship a mixed-strategy blueprint as typed arrays, backed by a deterministic feature encoder and a bounded, range-aware fallback. Train the six-seat preflop policy and common postflop lines offline. Add opponent adjustments only where they improve held-out results. Use the same decision path in Node and the browser. Keep live subgame solving out of that path.

**Target.** Six seats, 5/10 blinds, 1,000-chip (100 bb) starting stacks, one CPU core, plain TypeScript/JavaScript, no GPU or network during play. Benchmark a rake-free cash table reset to 100 bb each hand to control exposure. If the simulator carries stacks forward, assess that mode separately and measure coverage across stack depths. The repository’s [table rules](../rules.md) are the authority for legal actions and payouts.

**Evidence.** Published results establish that offline policies can play strong heads-up poker and that search improves some six-player systems. The sources below do not establish strong six-max play at 100 µs in JS. The proposed training and timing ranges are engineering estimates. Earning that strength claim requires a matchup benchmark against credible six-max policies.

## 1. Landscape

### CFR as a policy compiler

All of these solvers can export the same runtime artifact. For a decision with a known row, choose among a small number of actions in O(number of actions) time. The training algorithm changes coverage, quality and cost. It need not change inference cost.

| Training method | What happens offline | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CFR | Traverse the tree, update counterfactual regrets and accumulate a reach-weighted average strategy. Full traversal enumerates chance and player branches. | Use for tiny correctness games and tightly restricted subgames. Full six-max traversal is outside the budget. |
| CFR+ | Regret-matching+ clips cumulative regrets at zero, typically with alternating updates and a delayed or weighted average. Published speedups apply to the games and implementations tested. | Good for small, dense offline subgames. Copying the clipping rule directly into a noisy sampled trainer changes its behavior. |
| Discounted CFR (DCFR) | Discount old positive regrets, negative regrets and strategy sums on separate schedules. Linear CFR weights later iterations more heavily and is a useful simpler comparison. | Compare on fixed wall time. For a sparse trainer, account for elapsed discounts on cold rows or implement explicit periodic passes. |
| Chance-sampled CFR | Sample chance events such as a deal and explore player actions for those outcomes. Public-chance sampling can share a public board across vectorized private states. | Useful when betting branches are small. Sampling chance alone leaves the six-way betting explosion. |
| External-sampling MCCFR | For each traverser update, sample chance and the other players’ actions and visit all available traverser actions. | First choice for a restricted six-seat blueprint. Work still grows with the traverser’s branching and repeated raises. |
| Outcome-sampling MCCFR | Follow one sampled terminal trajectory. Importance weighting and a sampling policy with support over relevant actions recover regret estimates. | Cheap per trajectory and higher variance in many settings. Keep as an experiment if traversal cost dominates. Measure progress per second. |

In multiplayer external sampling, implement and validate average-policy accounting explicitly. OpenSpiel exposes simple and full averaging modes. Check the sampling and reach weights for the chosen method before adding pruning or discounting. The two-player zero-sum guarantee relies on the appropriate regret and recall assumptions. Lossy public-state merging can discard earlier information and alter those assumptions. [CFR paper](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/NIPS07-cfr.pdf) · [CFR+](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5042) · [DCFR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04040) · [MCCFR paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2009/hash/00411460f7c92d2124a67ea0f4cb5f85-Abstract.html) · [OpenSpiel external sampling](https://github.com/google-deepmind/open_spiel/blob/master/open_spiel/algorithms/external_sampling_mccfr.h) · [Imperfect recall](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.0622)

### Recent work on cheaper play and training

- **AlphaHoldem (2022) is an earlier search-free reference point.** A single policy forward pass cut HU action time to a reported **2.9 ms** on a NVIDIA TITAN V. The authors trained for three days on one server with **8 GPUs and 64 CPU cores** and report **+11.16 ± 1.61 bb/100** against Slumbot. A much smaller model with fixed numeric features could fit our runtime, but its training and resulting strength would be different. [AlphaHoldem paper, introduction and Table 3](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/20394/20394-13-24407-1-2-20220628.pdf)
- **Baseline Poker’s 2023 HU blueprint** reportedly won **3.4 bb/100** against Slumbot over **100,000 hands** at 200 bb before the project shifted toward live solving. This is an author-reported datapoint for static policy strength. Its table size, training cost and latency were unverified. [Baseline Poker development history](https://baseline.poker/docs/description)
- **RL-CFR (ICML 2024)** learns which bet sizes to include, then solves with CFR. Reported **+8.4 ± 1.7 bb/100** against Slumbot in HU. Use the idea offline by allocating sizes to high-impact nodes. Published evidence gives no 100 µs six-max runtime. [RL-CFR proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/li24t.html)
- **EVPA (ICLR 2025)** uses trained value estimates for pruning and dynamic information abstraction. It reports **1–2% of solving time** compared with its DeepStack replication to reach similar solution quality in tested subgames. At a **20 ms** solving cap, EVPA-169 scored **+10 ± 26 mbb/hand** against Slumbot. At **200 ms**, EVPA-full scored **+96 ± 43 mbb/hand**. Even 20 ms is 200 times our allowance. [EVPA paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/8c1b5863a6b0f925617b917bb2f55be0-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **NexusGTO (2026)** is the closest recent same-format builder report. Its blueprint took **8.05 billion reported iterations** over roughly 11 days. An own-range-tracking search variant reports **+5.73 bb/100** (95% interval **+2.74 to +8.72**) against five blueprint copies across **14,250 duplicate deals** at 100 bb without rake. The project discloses that its decision rule did not select that variant as written. One six-way flop search benchmark dropped from 22 minutes to **13.4 s**, and the deployed configuration uses the blueprint at four or more remaining players. Its CC BY 4.0 preflop download is a candidate seed after verifying coverage and mapping. The results are self-reported and matchup-specific. [NexusGTO history and data license](https://nexusgto.com/history) · [Results](https://nexusgto.com/results)
- **Public 2026 implementations show what can be made cheap.** One C++ HU blueprint project reports a **37 s** abstraction build at 50/50/50 buckets on four threads, **329 MB peak**, and **14.9 million infoset visits/s** on one thread for its medium tree. It exports a table to TypeScript. A separate tournament-oriented Python project reports **~3 min on 10 cores** for equity tables, a **sub-1 MB** export and **170 µs p99** for its heuristic path on a loaded M5. Neither benchmark transfers directly to six-max JavaScript cash decisions. [C++ blueprint and TS export](https://github.com/hellomate2/gto-poker-overlay/blob/main/blueprint/README.md) · [Python experiment](https://github.com/JamesGongYC/nolimit-pokerbot)
- **Real-Time Parallel CFR (2026 preprint)** still reports approximately **47–54 ms for one postflop iteration** on a billion-history depth-limited HU tree with GPU leaf inference. **PokerSkill (2026 preprint)** saves game-specific training by pairing an LLM with poker skills, but still invokes an LLM in play. Both target different costs from this project. [Parallel CFR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19928) · [PokerSkill](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30094)

## 2. What to precompute on a laptop

### Betting representation

- **Preflop.** Keep the 169 suit-isomorphic starting-hand classes and six relative table positions. Preserve unopened, limped, open, 3-bet and 4-bet lines, whether hero is the aggressor, callers and players left to act, blind defense and raise size. Use opening sizes in BB (e.g. 2.2–2.5 bb, rounded to chips), position-appropriate reraises and jam. Retain additional sizes only where cross-play supports them.
- **Postflop.** Start from {fold, check/call, 0.33 pot, 0.66 pot, pot, all-in}. On many nodes offer one lead/bet size and one raise size, plus jam where distinct. Budget extra sizes for frequent lines and pressure points. Include a capped number of model raises per street and an explicit continuation or fallback at the cap. A vanilla six-action menu at every node still grows too large.
- **Use exact chips in the simulator.** Define raise fractions against the pot after matching the call. If current pot is 100 and calling costs 20, a pot-sized raise adds 120 above the call, so the actor puts in 140 more chips this turn. The engine resolves minimum raises, short all-ins, reopening and contribution accounting. Merge probability mass for actions that round or clamp to the same legal chip amount. Use exactly the same sizing semantics in training and inference.
- **Translate off-tree sizes in the policy only.** Bucket by exact amount relative to pot after call, all-in status and remaining stack. Start with adjacent-size interpolation where both rows exist, or a single deterministic mapped row when coverage is thin. Use the actual amount for pot odds and SPR. Route extreme overbets or incompatible action histories to a fallback. A formal alternative is pseudo-harmonic translation. [Action translation paper](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2013/0028.pdf)

### Card representation

| Feature | Meaning | Practical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EHS | Expected showdown pot share over future runouts and a specified opponent range. On the river it reduces to current showdown equity against that range. | Cheap scalar bucket after lookup or feature approximation. Preserve the range definition when comparing across runs. |
| EHS² | Average of squared river hand-strength values across completions. The expectation is outside the square. It distinguishes equal-mean hands with different upside and dispersion. | Include as an offline feature alongside mean equity. Similar EHS/EHS² pairs can still imply different action choices. |
| Potential-aware buckets | Cluster histograms of future strength or next-street buckets, often using earth-mover distance. | Sample representative boards offline and fit a cheap quantizer or threshold tree. Full histogram construction belongs outside inference. |
| OCHS | Opponent Cluster Hand Strength, a vector of strengths against clusters of opponent hands. | Start with a few reference classes such as tight value, broadway, pairs and suited hands. Fit a small proxy for play and keep blocker tags. A weighted dot product is useful for one opponent. In multiway pots, fit joint equity features or sample joint deals. |

Use 32–64 postflop buckets per street to start, with adaptive capacity where one bucket mixes strategically different hands. Key on street, board texture, made-hand tier, nut and draw potential, and a few blocker flags through a compact encoder. Bucket IDs should reflect the interaction of the two hole cards with the board. A board strength rank alone gives similar features to top pair on dry and coordinated boards. Estimate feature calibration on board families that were absent from training. [Johanson et al. on EHS² and OCHS](https://johanson.ca/publications/poker/2013-aamas-kmeans-abstraction/2013-aamas-kmeans-abstraction.pdf) · [Potential-aware abstraction](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/potential-aware_imperfect-recall.aaai14.pdf)

Exhaustive suit-canonical private/public state maps already have about **1.29 million flop**, **55.2 million turn** and **2.43 billion river** entries under Pluribus’s street-aware indexing. At one byte per entry that is about **2.5 GB** before indexing and strategy weights. Step up from a shared feature quantizer to dense flop IDs only if measured policy lift justifies it. Never store a full row per raw board and hero combo. [Pluribus supplement, information abstraction](https://noambrown.com/papers/19-Science-Superhuman_Supp.pdf)

### Offline schedule and policy assets

The following estimates are planning bounds for an optimized JS implementation on a recent laptop after the simulator exists. Development time is separate. Estimate each run from a 30–60 s pilot of actual **infoset visits/s**, with the real evaluator, abstraction and memory layout. A visit and a hand are different work units.

| Phase | Concrete work | Planning estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Validate the trainer | Kuhn/Leduc values and best-response checks. Enumerate legal tree sizes and coarse-state collisions. Benchmark compact traversal and sample accounting. | 1–10 min of runs |
| Prepare cards and baseline | Build combo and 169-class maps, deck masks, small range priors and representative card-feature samples. Fit buckets or use quantile thresholds. Start with 100k hero/board samples and about 32–64 showdowns each for sampled offline features, with stronger samples for validating the quantizer. | 5–30 min for feature generation and fitting as a first allocation |
| Train preflop | Keep six seats and the restricted betting/pot ledger. Sample full deals externally. At flop leaves use calibrated continuation values indexed by position, private class, pot type, number of players and SPR. Compare against a seed policy. | 15–60 min initial pass |
| Train common postflop lines | Sample preflop arrivals from seed/current policies. Train restricted single-raised, 3-bet and multiway limp pots. Preserve up to six eligible players at a root. Use actual runout/payout terminals in small river trees and frozen continuation estimates at cutoffs. Give more visits to high-frequency or high-stakes rows and track root weights so oversampling does not silently redefine the training objective. | 30 min–3 h initial pass |
| Export and select | Compare current snapshots, an average and the baseline in matched simulations. Export the best validated checkpoint and track unseen or low-visit rows. | 10–60 min initial allocation, with tournament wall time measured separately |

At an **assumed 0.2–1.0 million visits/s**, one core produces **0.72–3.6 billion visits per hour**. These are arithmetic planning assumptions, independent of the native C++ results. Coverage is uneven. Log average and lower-tail visit counts on the actual evaluation distribution, fallback rates and performance by snapshot. Frequency-weighted coverage matters more than raw visit count. If training is slow or unstable, trim raise branches and public contexts first, then test whether cutoffs or abstractions explain the lost strength. Keep preflop boundary values under review after the postflop policy changes. Restricted preflop plus separate postflop training approximates continuation values and loses strategic interactions across boundaries.

**Example export.** 400 preflop contexts × 169 classes × 6 action bytes plus 12,000 postflop contexts × 64 buckets × 6 action bytes is **5.01 MB (4.78 MiB)** of probabilities. An optional byte of support per row adds **0.84 MB**. This is a capacity example, not a promise that training covers those contexts. Use dense IDs within retained line families and sparse or sentinel offsets for missing keys. Quantize each row to probabilities summing to 255 by largest-remainder rounding, preserve intentional zeros, and check that rare strategic actions survive quantization. Add selectively higher-precision rows if the ablation shows loss. Keep Float32 regret and strategy sums offline. At six actions and two Float32 values per action, their dense counterparts consume **40.1 MB** before indexes and work buffers.

Package a versioned binary with magic, byte order, dimensions, sizing/feature version, offsets and checksum. Initialize `Uint8Array` probability and support sections and `Uint32Array` index sections once. Use a bundled local binary in Node or a build-generated embedded payload in the browser, decoded before simulation. Base64 expands an uncompressed payload by roughly one third. Benchmark startup and memory separately. Runtime lookups should use integer offsets and fixed scratch space. Persist the training configuration and source attribution for imported matrices in the eventual policy build.

## 3. Decision-time methods

| Method | Budget fit | Strategic role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct opening/reraise lookup | Excellent. A short key and a six-action row. | Foundation for 100 bb preflop. Preserve seat, prior actions and players left to act. |
| Bucket-indexed postflop lookup | Excellent if card classification uses fast masks and the public key updates incrementally. | Main mixed policy. Most quality depends on the representation and coverage. |
| Pot-odds and equity heuristics | Excellent with cheap features and an offline range-equity estimate. | Fallback for missing lines, sizing extremes and poorly supported rows. Retain value/bluff mixes. |
| Short showdown rollouts | Potentially viable in selected spots with a fixed sample count and fast evaluator. | Estimate equity for a bounded correction. Future betting EV and actual showdown equity can diverge. |
| Full betting-tree rollouts or CFR at the decision | Poor under this ceiling. | Generate teacher data offline. Hard determinization can produce strategy fusion by letting a simulated actor act as if it knew hidden cards. |

**Pot odds and betting.** If `P` is the current pot including bets already made, `C` is the call, and the call closes action with one eligible pot and no future betting or rake, break-even equity is `C / (P + C)`. For example, 50 into a pot now holding 150 needs 25% equity. With future betting, account for position and equity realization. In side pots, compute expected collectible payout across the pots hero can win, minus additional investment. For a zero-equity heads-up bluff risking `B` to win `P0` before the bet, immediate break-even fold frequency is `B / (P0 + B)`. The complementary `P0 / (P0 + B)` is the simple heads-up minimum defense frequency (MDF) in that model. It is a range-level diagnostic. In multiway play the bettor needs everyone to fold, so per-seat MDF targets overdefend and independent fold products can mislead.

**Range-aware corrections.** Start with position and line priors. Maintain a compact vector of opponent style weights or bucket likelihoods and update it on observed actions. A practical likelihood model multiplies prior weights by a smoothed `Pr(action | bucket, context, style)` and normalizes. Filter blockers consistently and shrink toward priors with little evidence. Use opponent-specific fold/call tendencies and expected equity when called to tilt a few action probabilities, e.g. move no more than five percentage points of mass initially. Require evidence before expanding the cap. Keep preflop probabilities and thin river bluff/call choices conservative where the model is uncertain. Off-tree and low-support fallback rows can use stronger rules because they lack a reliable learned policy.

**Cheap rollouts.** Use prebuilt weighted combo lists, fixed scratch buffers and cap both valid samples and collision retries. Opponents’ holdings and runouts must be disjoint from hero, the board and one another. Sequentially rejecting only the later seat’s blocked combos creates an order-dependent approximation to joint range conditioning. If targeting exact product weights conditional on a legal deal, jointly draw and reject entire conflicting assignments or use a validated conditional sampler. Use one world per comparison across candidate lines if doing a bounded EV estimate. On the river with one opponent, hero and board leave `C(45,2) = 990` possible opponent combos before range filtering. Exact enumeration may work for a narrow effective range after measurement. Keep it off the default path.

The repository’s [evaluator notes](../performance.md#4-optional-equity-for-known-hole-cards) report **2.85–2.88 million completed boards/s for six fixed known hands** on an Apple M5 Pro in Node. Six-way unknown-weighted sampling adds rejection, range sampling and potential side-pot work. Start with at most **16–32** samples for an optional late-street correction and profile it separately. For bounded pot share, the worst-case standard error is **12.5 percentage points at 16** samples and **8.8 at 32**. Use a prior or a confidence-aware small adjustment. Near break-even, that sample count cannot justify flipping a decision confidently.

## 4. Multiway changes the objective

Nash equilibrium is defined for finite six-player poker, and such a game has a mixed equilibrium. The difficulty is that its total chip payoff sums to zero while each player faces several distinct incentives. One player’s unilateral deviation can benefit another at a third player’s expense. Equilibria may have different payoffs and strategies from different equilibrium profiles need not combine into an equilibrium. The independently averaged strategies of ordinary CFR in multiplayer self-play have no general Nash convergence guarantee. Evaluate field performance and unilateral response probes instead of treating a small training regret number as an exploitability certificate. [Multiplayer CFR results](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0034) · [Pluribus paper](https://noambrown.com/papers/19-Science-Superhuman.pdf)

Pluribus used self-play against five copies, then added depth-limited search with multiple continuation choices at the leaves. Each remaining player, including the searching player, could choose among blueprint continuations biased toward folding, calling or raising, or the original blueprint. The choices adjust the upstream strategy to multiple plausible later policies. Earlier abstractions and blueprint reach support those searches. The paper reports **+48 mbb/hand (4.8 bb/100)** with standard error **25 mbb/hand** across **10,000 hands** of one bot against five professionals, using AIVAT and a one-tailed significance test. This reports the combined system’s outcome. [Pluribus paper and supplement](https://noambrown.com/papers/19-Science-Superhuman.pdf) · [Search and continuation details](https://noambrown.com/papers/19-Science-Superhuman_Supp.pdf)

For a fast policy, preserve who can still act, who can win each pot, relative position, initiative, effective stacks against relevant opponents and the difference between active and all-in players. In later three-way pots, the bettor, caller and player behind often have very different ranges. Calibrate bluffs against the joint all-fold rate. Treat raw heads-up equity as a feature only, and price the possibility of overcalls or reraises before assuming an immediate call closes action. Fit distinct policies for HU postflop reached from a six-seat table and genuinely multiway postflop. Both depend on how the hand reached that street.

## 5. Recommended architecture and acceptance

### 1. Six-max static blueprint with bounded corrections

| Module | Responsibility | Estimated warmed budget per decision |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| State adapter | Incremental public history, relative seat masks, exact pot and call, SPR bands, legal action view from the simulator | 1–5 µs |
| Card features | 169-class preflop map or postflop masks, strength/draw/texture/blocker feature extraction and fast bucket assignment | 1–10 µs |
| Policy lookup | Resolve exact or translated public contexts, read at most two quantized rows and blend or fall back | 1–5 µs |
| Fallback and bounded adjustment | Cheap range weights, pot odds and equity proxies, with a fixed cap on adjustment and compute | 2–12 µs |
| Sampling and action projection | Merge aliases, mask illegal actions, normalize, sample with a seeded PRNG and emit exact integer chips | 1–4 µs |
| **Default path planning envelope** | Base policy without optional rollouts | **6–36 µs** |
| Optional late-street correction | Range-aware showdown trials with a strict work cap, enabled only after separate tests | **Allocate up to 40 µs more** |

These are design allocations, not sum-of-percentile predictions. Begin with the default path and the lowest-complexity opponent priors. Measure expensive branches with realistic allocations, varied cards and cache locality. Keep UI messaging, logs and persistence outside the decisions. In browsers, initialization and long simulations can live in one worker on one core, with batched progress updates. Leave margin for JIT, GC and device variance.

### 2. Preflop charts plus a postflop feature policy

Start from compatible six-max mixed preflop charts or basic generated priors. Use pot odds, SPR, coarse equity versus position-dependent ranges, texture, blockers and capped opponent adaptations postflop. Fit a small linear scorer, threshold tree or compact MLP to offline rollout EVs or teacher action distributions on common lines. A 40 → 32 → 6 MLP has **1,472 weight multiply-adds**. That can plausibly fit the budget in plain JS with preallocated arrays, subject to feature extraction and sampling cost. Use mixed teacher targets to retain bluff and value frequencies. Plan **15–90 min** for an initial offline target/tuning run with fixed cheap teachers and a small feature representation. Training cost changes sharply if labels come from full solvers. This is a faster baseline and useful fallback policy, with greater dependence on the quality of teacher data and handcrafted features.

### Promotion gates

1. **Engine and information boundaries.** Test integer payouts, short raises, reopening, ties, eligible pots, off-menu actions and depleted stacks. Verify that the policy receives only its own hole cards and public information. Keep opponent private cards accessible only to the simulator and authorized offline analysis.
2. **Speed.** Measure one warmed Node thread and one browser worker on representative hardware. Replay varied six-seat traces with preflop, postflop, table misses, off-tree bets, side pots and multiway spots. Include the state encoding, policy and legal action output, plus any per-action opponent maintenance. Report aggregate throughput, p50/p95/p99/max and per-branch counts. Require **≥10,000 decisions/s** and target **p99 ≤100 µs** to defend the latency budget. Put a deterministic bound on algorithmic work because the JS scheduler can still pause. Report cold startup and whole-simulator hands/s separately. At 10,000 decisions/s, 50,000 hands at an illustrative 12 total bot decisions each take **60 s of bot decision time**, plus engine and harness overhead.
3. **Strength.** Use at least **50,000 hands** of matched games for the initial evaluation. Rotate seats, reuse seeded deals across candidate/control and group uncertainty by duplicate-deal blocks or independent sessions if opponent state carries across hands. Report bb/100 and 95% intervals by lineup and for pooled held-out play. AIVAT can help when its policy and value-model inputs are available. Inspect head-to-head segments, 3+ players remaining segments and weak spots under off-menu sizes, squeeze pressure and overbets. [AIVAT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06915)
4. **Field quality and ablations.** Compare against independent or clearly different strong local opponents, including a rival blueprint or vetted range policy and specialized pressure variants.
