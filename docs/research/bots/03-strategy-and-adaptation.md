# Strategy and opponent adaptation for a fast 6-max NLHE bot

The strongest design for this simulator is a **precomputed mixed strategy with a small, confidence-gated adaptation layer**. Solve or distill the high-traffic decisions offline. Use compact rules for uncovered positions, sizes, boards and stack depths. At runtime, select a table row, apply a bounded adjustment, filter for legality and sample once. Use an adaptation-off copy as the control in every experiment.

**Game assumptions.** Six seats, blinds 5/10, 1,000 chips at the start of a 100bb hand, no ante and no rake. Chip EV is the objective. SB vs BB is heads-up with SB out of position postflop when four players fold. Reduced-seat games use the actual button and action order. Use effective stacks **before posting** to index preflop depth and remaining effective stacks to calculate postflop SPR. Store amounts in integer chips. A preflop raise size in this report means **raise to** that amount, including the player's existing street contribution. See [simulator rules](../rules.md).

**Evidence.** Published numbers below retain their solve conditions. The proposed ranges, percentages and confidence thresholds are starter parameters for this simulator. A full six-player no-rake solve, strategic benchmark and latency measurements are still required to substantiate near-GTO play or a 100 µs runtime guarantee.

**Choose the hands offline.** Seed the 3-bet value ordering with QQ+/AK versus early opens, adding JJ/AQs versus CO and TT/AJs/AQo against BTN in the blinds. Treat these as ordering cues and let the solved row decide the mix. A 7% row is about 93 combos and QQ+/AK provides only 34 before blockers. In positions with few calls, use the next tier of hands for most of the rest, prioritizing JJ–99, strong suited broadways and suited aces, with a smaller wheel-ace contribution. Where a meaningful calling range exists, reserve playable middle hands for it and fill a portion of the raises from blocker hands like A5s–A2s and selected suited connectors or gappers. For cold-calls, rank remaining hands by continuation EV and penalize marginal offsuit broadways and hands vulnerable to squeezes. BTN vs CO can start with mixed 66–99, ATs–AJs, KJs–KQs, QJs, JTs, T9s and 98s. Include some strong hands in call rows when a solve supports it. A single ranking by all-in equity makes poor postflop ranges.

**How to fit a row without a solve.** Reserve the value tier first, then select mixed 3-bets from a separate list of blocker/coverage candidates. For the remaining call mass, use offline equity against the opener's weighted RFI range and use an equity-realization estimate by class, position and players behind. A concrete fallback score in bb is `R[class,spot] × e × (P+C) − C − squeezeTax`, with `P` the current pot, `C` the extra call and `e` including ties. Initialize `R` at 0.88 IP or 0.78 OOP, add 0.07 for suited or paired, subtract 0.07 for dominated offsuit broadways/aces when the opener is early, and start `squeezeTax` at 0.10bb per live player behind, zero for a closing BB. These are ranking coefficients, not independent guarantees of positive EV. Prefer positive score rows. Use capped, distinct class lists for aggressive and passive actions, skip unreachable classes, and mix the marginal class to approximate the target rather than forcing the volume into poor hands. Replace scores with measured continuation EVs. Build with quantized sizes such as 2/2.5/3/4bb and preserve the actual price when playing an in-between size.

**Stack-off price.** In 2 → 9 → 21 → 100bb with 0.5bb dead, the 4-bettor calls 79bb for a final 200.5bb pot and needs **39.4%** equity. Estimate against the *jam* range. In a heads-up jam decision, using current pot `P` including the 4-bet, extra jam cost `J`, opponent's additional call `C_v` and fold rate `f`, `EVjam = fP + (1−f)[e(P+J+C_v)−J]`. ### Squeezes, limps and short stacks

- **Squeeze.** Use the 3-bet size plus roughly one initial open amount per caller. For 2.5bb + one call, start at 10.5–11bb IP and 12.5–13.5bb OOP. Start near 6–8% versus an early opener plus one caller and 9–13% versus late open plus late call. Use QQ+/AK as an early core, widening to TT+/AQs+ and selected A5s against loose ranges. Trim the bluff portion when more callers or a tight opener reduce joint folds. BB can overcall suited/nut-capable hands at favorable prices. ART/GTO illustrates SB squeezing 6.9%, 7.9% and 8.8% after UTG, HJ or CO opens with a BTN caller, respectively. [SB squeeze rows](https://artgto.com/learn/ranges/sb/)
- **Limp handling.** Default to no open limp outside SB at 100bb. Against one limper start with **4bb IP / 5bb OOP**, add 1bb per additional limper. Narrow to stronger value when callers are sticky and preserve nut potential in overlimps. BB checks for free with marginal holdings when allowed. Versus a lone SB completion, start with a 3.5–4bb BB iso and roughly 30% raises as a calibration seed, including strong high cards, pairs and mixed suited blockers. Check the remaining hands. If adding an SB limp branch, solve raise/complete/fold jointly. A first test can transfer 20% of the AA/KK/AKs raise probability into completes, add completes to the unused probability of some weak suited and connected hands, and use the premium completes as a limp-reraise branch. BB's iso response needs its own row. - **Short stacks.** Build distinct effective-depth rows around 10, 15, 20, 30, 50, 75 and 100bb. At 15–30bb use small opens and solve for rejam/call alongside small reraises. At roughly **10bb and below**, use [HoldemResources' no-ante heads-up Nash charts](https://www.holdemresources.net/hune) as a fallback when only SB/BB remain and the available strategy is simplified to shove/fold vs call/fold. The chart's thresholds approximate mixed strategies. For UTG–BTN, multiple behind, antes, reshoves or unequal stacks, solve separate chip-EV tables with live-seat and stack buckets. Keep min-open and limp branches at shallow depths where the game allows them.

## 2. Postflop strategy as tables and rules

### Price, defense and bluff composition

Let `P` be the pot before the first heads-up bet, `B` the bet and `s=B/P`. A terminal call needs equity `B/(P+2B)`. A zero-equity bluff that always loses when continued against and has a zero-EV check breaks even at `alpha=B/(P+B)`. The toy-game minimum defense frequency is `MDF=1−alpha`, where raises count as defense. Use MDF as a diagnostic in a heads-up toy game with an available zero-equity bluff of the stated size. Coverage, equity realization, rake, future action and advantage can move the equilibrium defense away from MDF. For an arbitrary call, use the amount still owed divided by the pot **after that call**. For a pure bluff raise that loses its full new investment if anyone continues, use `alpha=extraChipsRisked/(potNow+extraChipsRisked)`, where the pot now includes chips already committed, including the facing bet. Use eligible pots only and account for uncalled returns and future action. [MDF and alpha](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mdf-alpha/) · [Limitations](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mathematical-misconceptions-in-poker/)

\* **Toy value-to-bluff ratios within one bet size.** On the river, against a pure bluff-catcher that always loses to value, the indifferent bluff share is `q=s/(1+2s)`, giving `V:B=(1+s):s`. Earlier columns use a static, perfectly polarized multi-street toy model with the same size available each street, no raises and value always betting. After a call the bettor gives up a fraction `q` of the prior betting mass on the next street to leave the bluff-catcher indifferent. The current value fraction is the product of `(1−q_i)` across the remaining bets, including this one. Thus three 75% bets give flop value share `0.7³=34.3%`. Flop and turn ratios are calibration examples, with no universal equilibrium quota for real boards. Draw equity, changing nuts, checking strong hands, different sizes and merges all change the mix. A 75% turn shove uses the terminal column for identical polar assumptions. [Pot geometry and polarized models](https://blog.gtowizard.com/pot-geometry/)

**Semi-bluff screening.** For an all-in or simplified single-call checkdown model, use `EVbet=fP+(1−f)[e_called(P+2B)−B]`. At 75% pot with 25% equity if called, the called branch is `−0.125P`, and about 11.1% folds beat a zero baseline. If checking has positive EV, demand more. Exact card-only outs from flop with `o` clean, fixed outs give `1−(47−o)(46−o)/(47×46)` to hit by river, and `o/46` from turn. Nine flush outs give 35.0% and 19.6%, respectively. Eight open-ended straight outs give 31.5% and 17.4%. Discount dirty outs, duplicate outs, domination and losses to redraws. Use next-card equity when a future bet can force a fold before the river. Prioritize draws with nut potential or a valuable blocker, then check back medium draws that would lose realization to a raise.

### Features and frequency selection

Build range and nut summaries **from the two weighted ranges after the actual action line**. Mean heads-up equity is a range-advantage signal. Nut advantage uses the mass of very strong hands each range can hold, such as sets/two pair on dry flops or the best straights and flushes on completed boards. Split the latter into absolute nut and near-nut buckets with board-aware rules. Compute reach-weighted strengths and disjoint combo equity offline. Use suit-canonical board IDs, prior betting sequence, relative position, active players, SPR, hand strength, draw class and suit/rank blockers. A monotone K95 and a monotone 876 need separate rows. Use low-cost board fallback features such as high card, pair/trips, suit count, ranks in possible straight windows and a turn/river nut-change bit. There are 1,755 suit-isomorphic flops; fine board keys on common heads-up lines can be practical. [Flop grouping and range effects](https://blog.gtowizard.com/flop-heuristics-ip-c-betting-in-cash-games/)

A concrete starter calibration uses **+5 percentage points in mean equity** as a substantial range edge, and **+3 points in top-bucket mass or a 1.5× ratio with at least 5% own top-bucket mass** as a substantial nuts edge. Use these to choose candidate bets, then fit the mixing weights offline. High mean equity with equal top-end strength typically supports frequent small bets. A top-end edge supports larger polar bets. Where both players have many strong hands or the defender has more nuts, protect the checking range and lower frequency. Preflop initiative and wetness alone are insufficient. **Check-raise.** From BB after checking heads-up against a 25–33% flop c-bet, start around **10–18% of reached hands**, **15–25%** on selected low paired or favorable draw boards and **5–10%** versus a 75% bet. Use about **3.5–4.5×** a small bet or **2.7–3.3×** a larger bet as a raise-to size. Use sets, trips and two pair plus strong draws, selected backdoors on dry textures and occasional vulnerable strong pairs where denial pays. Keep strong calls and some protected checks. Start **5–12%** on turn vs typical heads-up bets when a value/draw range exists. River check-raises start at **0–5%**, polarized and driven by actual combo mass and blockers. Keep these as broad initial bands because a small bet and available nut hands can change the equilibrium raise rate sharply. **Donk, probe and delay.** A donk leads into the prior aggressor before that player acts. Default to near-zero flop leads, with a **10–25% at 25–33% pot** branch on selected low boards that give the BB real nut coverage. GTO Wizard's 7♠5♥3♦ BTN vs BB example leads about **25%**, mainly small. After the flop checks through, the OOP caller's turn lead is a probe. Start at **20–40% / 33–75% pot** when the turn favors the caller and a checked-back range has many vulnerable hands, and **5–20%** on turns that restore the IP edge. For the IP raiser's delayed c-bet after a checked-through flop and another check, start **30–55% / 33–75% pot** if the turn preserves or increases its advantage. On a range-flipping turn after check/call, use a separate turn-donk node. Begin with a low-frequency 20–33% size, adding 75–100% only when the caller gains a substantial nut edge. ### Turn, river and SPR

**Geometric sizing.** In a heads-up pot with remaining effective stack `S`, pot `P` and `n` betting streets including this one, equal pot fractions `g` grow the pot by `(1+2g)^n` when called every time. Setting final pot to `P+2S` gives `g=((1+2×SPR)^(1/n)−1)/2`, with `SPR=S/P`. At SPR 4 with two streets left, use 100% then 100%. At SPR 2 with three streets left, use about **35.5%** each. A BTN 2.5bb open called by BB makes a 5.5bb flop pot and ~17.7 SPR, about **116%** each to jam by river if playing geometric. A 9bb 3-bet pot between BTN and BB with dead SB has 18.5bb and ~4.9 SPR, about **61%** each. Prefer this plan on static boards with a strong nut edge. Vulnerable value may choose a larger earlier bet, and thin value often chooses smaller. Round to a legal size, recompute the next street with the actual pot and include all-in as a distinct action. [Pot geometry](https://blog.gtowizard.com/pot-geometry/) · [Hypergeometric sizing](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-power-of-the-hypergeometric-bet/)

**Turn.** Reclassify the board after each new card and condition on the flop line. On a blank or a card that improves the aggressor's range, start **40–65% barreling** with 50–75%, geometric or 125–150% on a true nut-edge branch. On opposing range shifts start **15–35%**, usually a check or a selective small bet. Reserve **125–150%** for a strong top end plus convincing bluffs when the defender's line is relatively capped, with enough stack to support the intended river line. Check medium strength that has little value against continuing hands. Maintain strong combinations in the checking range. At SPR below 1, compare a legal shove to a small bet/check directly. High SPR demands more polarized stack-offs. Position and runout stability matter as much as SPR.

**Pot control.** Give a medium made hand a check-back preference when much of the worse range folds, the better range continues and few turn or river cards threaten its equity. On a stable turn with middle pair or weak top pair, compare the EV of checking and bluff-catching a river bet against a thin 25–33% value bet and its raise branch. As a fallback, check **60–85%** of this hand bucket IP at SPR ≥4. Increase small bets when worse pairs or live overcards continue often enough to pay for value or denial. Carry some strong hands through the same check line to keep profitable river defense available.

**River.** Use exact terminal pot odds when closing the action, evaluated against the posterior **betting range** for call and the **continuing range** for bet. For an IP showdown check, `EVcheck=e_check×P`. Evaluate 25–50% thin value, 75–100% value/polar, 125–200% polar and jam using fold, call and raise branches. A call-only approximation uses the bet EV formula above. Include net EV after our response to a raise in the raise branch. With no raises and all folds coming from hands we already beat, thin value requires winning over half the called mass, counting ties as half. If better hands fold, score the whole bet instead. OOP small block bets only make sense if the raise branch has acceptable EV and strong hands protect the small size. For each polar size, build value first and select bluffs by available weighted combo mass. Example for 75% pot: 14 value combos support 6 terminal bluff combos in the toy ratio. With ties, mixed strategies and thin value this is a cross-check. [Value and sizing examples](https://blog.gtowizard.com/whats_your_hand_worth_from_thin_value_to_playing_for_stacks/) · [MDF and alpha](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mdf-alpha/)

**Blockers.** Rank low-showdown river bluffs by their removal of hands that call or raise and by how well they leave folds unblocked. One offline score is `removedContinueMass/continueMass − removedFoldMass/foldMass`, with safe denominators. Weight different continuation classes by the EV of their response if available. An A♠ can block nut flushes on a three-spade river while a missed draw can remove the exact hands you want to fold on a blank. When bluff-catching, favor removing value while leaving missed bluffs available. For value, prefer combos that leave worse calls available. Materialize a small signed blocker score or refine the suit-specific policy row offline. [Showdown-based exploit examples](https://blog.gtowizard.com/building-exploits-from-showdown/)

**Multiway.** Start by reducing the heads-up bet frequency by **15–30 percentage points** and the pure-bluff component by **50–75%**, then fit dedicated three-way rows with position and relative action order. Use stronger value and nuttier draws, check medium hands, and avoid firing repeatedly into several uncapped ranges. Small size is often efficient, while some nut-advantaged lines justify big bets. Upgrade the call threshold for squeeze/reopen exposure. Avoid applying MDF to each defender. Against two equal independent defenders facing a half-pot bluff, total fold probability must reach ⅓, which corresponds to each folding about 57.7% if their responses were independent. Real actions are conditional, so estimate joint folds as a product of **conditional** fold rates in order or fit the joint outcome directly. For side pots, sum expected shares of each pot the hand can win and subtract its additional contribution. Retain live players who can act again in the state. [Multiway defense and sizing](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/) · [Relative position examples](https://blog.gtowizard.com/monkey-in-the-middle-3-way-pot-heuristics/)

## 3. Opponent models and bounded exploitation

### Count opportunities by meaningful context

| Stat | Numerator / denominator | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VPIP | Hands with a voluntary preflop call or raise / dealt hands with a VPIP opportunity | Loose/tight signal. Exclude forced blinds/antes and BB walks. |
| PFR | Hands with any preflop raise / dealt non-walk hands for this implementation | Track separate RFI by seat. A large VPIP–PFR gap suggests passive entries. |
| 3-bet | Hands or spot events that 3-bet / opportunities facing exactly one raise where a reraise is legal | Split by opener and defender seats, size and cold-callers. Track squeezes separately. |
| Fold to 3-bet | Folds / times our observed player opened and then faced a 3-bet | Split by IP/OOP and size. Record continuation type as call or 4-bet. |
| C-bet | Continuation bets / opportunities as prior-street aggressor, with no intervening lead | Split by street, IP/OOP, player count, pot class and texture. For turn/river use distinct continuation, delayed and probe labels. |
| Fold to c-bet | Folds / eligible c-bets faced | Split by street, size and texture. Earlier calls change the reached range on later streets. |
| AF | (Bets + raises) / calls | Highly unstable when calls are rare. Report undefined at zero calls or smooth both count streams. |
| AFq | (Bets + raises) / (bets + raises + calls + folds) | Conventional definition excludes checks. Also track `bet / (bet+check)` at eligible first-bet nodes. |
| WTSD | Showdowns / flops seen | Indirect evidence of calling down. Split at least HU/multiway. |
| W$SD | Showdowns paying the player any pot share / showdowns | Sensitive to hand selection and runouts. Declare the tie and side-pot convention and keep a separate chip-share measure if needed. |
| Check-raise | Raises after checking / times faced a bet after checking and could legally raise | Split street and size. Separate raises facing a bet from cold reraises and opportunities without sufficient stack. |

Count first eligible decisions for each line-specific denominator. Use per-action events for AF and AFq. Keep the conventions consistent when comparing to an external HUD, since definitions such as walks and ties can differ. Observe all public actions by all seats. Exclude hidden information provided by the simulator except when revealed at the table. [PokerTracker statistical reference](https://www.pokertracker.com/guides/PT3/general/statistical-reference-guide) · [PokerTracker HUD guide](https://docs.pokertracker.com/pt4/tutorials/the-hud/basic-hud-guide/)

**Shrinkage.** Treat each yes/no event as a beta-binomial cell. With node baseline `p0` and prior weight `k`, use `a0=k×p0`, `b0=k×(1−p0)` and posterior mean `p̂=(a0+y)/(k+n)`. Clip estimation priors to 0.01–0.99 when the event is legal, leaving impossible actions at zero. Start with `k=30–60` for common preflop cells and `k=50–100` for noisy, narrowly segmented postflop cells. For fold/call/raise and sizing, prefer a single Dirichlet row with pseudocount mass `k×p0[action]` to keep action mass normalized. Back off from opponent × position × line × size × texture to opponent × line and then population/blueprint. Fit the parent prior on disjoint training data, a cross-fitted parent, or a joint hierarchical model. Avoid counting the same actions twice. A broad parent can regularize a rare line while contributing no direct observations to that line's exploit gate. Use a frozen baseline matched by position/board/size distribution for testing. [Bayes' Bluff](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1411) · [Data-biased robust counter-strategies](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v5/johanson09a.html)

**Sample size.** For independent Bernoulli observations near 50%, the approximate two-sided 95% half-width is `0.98/√n`. The rows below are conservative planning figures for stationary independent opportunities. Use posterior quantiles and account for clustering or decay in practice.

| Desired half-width | Opportunities needed | Total observed hands at 20% / 5% / 1% opportunity |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ±10 percentage points | 97 | 485 / 1,940 / 9,700 |
| ±5 percentage points | 385 | 1,925 / 7,700 / 38,500 |
| ±3 percentage points | 1,068 | 5,340 / 21,360 / 106,800 |

At 50,000 hands, a 1% event gives only about 500 samples for a stable opponent before further splits or decay. As a concrete planning example, if the target sees a BTN open from BB on 4% of dealt hands, that yields about 2,000 events. If a particular river line occurs on 0.2% of hands, it yields only about 100 samples. Use roughly 500 observed hands for a first VPIP/PFR read and actual *opportunity counts* for every conditional stat. Predeclare the major leak cells or use holdouts or false-discovery control for a broad scan. Repeatedly checking overlapping windows and choosing the biggest outlier inflates false alarms.

### Convert frequencies to actionable range estimates

1. Start each opponent from the reachable strategy row, conditional on their position, prior line and effective depth. Initialize each combo with its dealt weight. Zero combos blocked by the board or hero's hole cards, and account for other live ranges offline through disjoint-card sampling or solving.
2. Offline, build shape variants for a wide or tight opener, linear/polar 3-bettor, sticky caller, c-bet-heavy/weak checker, and over- or underbluffing river range. Increase or decrease the *marginal* mass while preserving a believable value core and suit coverage. A 12% 3-bet statistic can come from very different hands, so select a **mixture** of plausible shapes. Showdowns can move weights between those hypotheses. They are selected by prior actions, cards and the propensity to show. Use a modeled reveal likelihood or treat them as weak evidence.
3. Given visible action `a`, update `w'(h) ∝ w(h) × Pr(a|h,node,model)` and remove newly impossible combos on each board card. As a cheap binary action calibration, choose an offline log-odds offset `λ` so the weighted average of `sigmoid(logit(p0(h))+λ)` matches the shrunk rate for that node. Keep small floors on plausible legal actions, and use a multinomial analogue for competing actions. A shift matches frequency without identifying hand selection. For runtime, map shrunk stats into quantized range-template IDs and advance those IDs by action, with precomputed feature/EV summaries. Any combo-level fitting stays outside the decision path.
4. Compare against the *reachable baseline*. A 70% c-bet can be high on 986 two-tone after a single raise and low in a favorable 3-bet pot. A high VPIP on its own does not identify a BB overfold. Observed river bet rate is not the bluff rate. Infer the latter from hand-selection models, censored showdowns and direct evidence in that line. [Bayes' Bluff](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1411) · [Building exploits from showdown](https://blog.gtowizard.com/building-exploits-from-showdown/)

### Safety, confidence and adaptation

| Method | How it works | Application here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restricted Nash Response (RNR) | Solve a modified game where a hidden root draw fixes the opponent to a model with probability `p`, and lets the opponent respond adversarially otherwise. | Offline only. Use a grid such as `p=0, .10, .25, .50` in restricted games and retain responses that meet loss limits. |
| Data-biased response (DBR) | Replace the single model-confidence constant with an information-set-dependent restriction `p_conf(I)` when modeling the opponent's choices. | Offline template design. Borrow the evidence-sensitive weighting for online selection. Missing cells get confidence zero. |
| ε-bounded response | If the baseline and counter-policy have certified losses `δ` and `L` relative to value `v` in a two-player zero-sum abstraction, a private ex-ante strategy draw mixing in the counter-policy with probability `w` has worst-case loss at most `(1−w)δ+wL`. | For `L>δ`, cap `w≤(ε−δ)/(L−δ)` when `δ≤ε`. E.g. `δ=.5, L=5, ε=1` bb/100 gives a cap of 11.1%. Applies within the game and assumptions used to certify the two losses. |
| Online clipped adjustments | Move probability mass between tested adjacent actions when both a directional confidence gate and an EV gate pass. | Cheap default. The formal mixture bound does not transfer to independently clipping many nodes or to six-player opponent models. Measure loss empirically. |

RNR and DBR were evaluated in smaller/two-player poker settings. Nodelocking is useful for generating exploit candidates. Include adversarial or partially unlocked opponents before shipping them, since a fully locked model can be overfit. Six-player play has no direct equivalent of the two-player zero-sum Nash safety guarantee. Test one adaptive seat and coordinated failure cases separately. [RNR paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2007/file/6e7b33fdea3adc80ebd648fffb665bb8-Paper.pdf) · [DBR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v5/johanson09a.html) · [Nodelocking](https://blog.gtowizard.com/introducing-nodelocking/) · [Pluribus multiplayer discussion](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aay2400)

**Suggested shipping gate.** Require at least **100 effective direct opportunities** at the matching node for a routine adjustment, **300** for rare high-cost raise/river/all-in adjustments, and a one-sided **95%** posterior bound beyond `p0 ± 5` percentage points in the useful direction. Also require conservative estimated marginal EV to beat the current action by **0.02bb per decision** or a calibrated estimator error floor, whichever is larger. Test plausible range shapes and use the adverse posterior rate. Require two independent evaluation blocks or confirmed evidence on held-out seeds before raising the cap. With gates passed, start with at most **5 percentage points of probability mass moved per row**, up to **10 points** after validation, and cap the union of cooperating adjustments in the same line. Spend the mass mainly on near-indifferent hands. Avoid spending it all on one “tell” size. Example: at 75% pot, if the conservative fold bound is 52%, then a zero-equity bluff against fold-or-continue yields at least `0.52P − 0.48×0.75P = 0.16P` before comparison to checking. A weak bound above a toy threshold by itself cannot justify a costly strategic change.

**Time variation.** Track a long estimate over roughly **8,000 observed hands half-life** and a fast estimate over **1,000–1,500**. Recompute outside the action path. For event weight `w_i=2^(−age_i/H)`, accumulate `W=Σw_i`, `Y=Σw_i x_i`, and `n_eff=W²/Σw_i²`. Treat recent information as a power-likelihood approximation, with effective data mass `n_data=min(W,n_eff)` before any block-correlation reduction. Update with successes `n_data×Y/W` and failures `n_data×(1−Y/W)` where `W>0`, so sparse stale cells lose influence. For a drift alarm, compare **disjoint** recent and previous windows matched for node mix. As a seed, require 50 effective direct events in each, a **10-point** change, and magnitude above **2.5 combined standard errors** for two separate comparisons. For low-frequency spots, use coarse parent diagnostics and freeze related rare exploits until evidence recovers. On alarm fade overlays toward zero, preserve the long-run baseline and re-enable only after fresh confirmation. An opponent's fold-to-3-bet may change specifically after our wider 3-bet regime begins. Track responses to hero and to other seats separately, with size and hero policy version held comparable. Detect a distribution shift first and attribute it to adaptation only when the change tracks our policy and persists under matched conditions.

**Resist counter-adaptation.** Use private random draws, fixed seeds only for controlled reproduction and independent streams per seat/match. Mix every materially close action according to the policy, preserving strong checks, strong calls and balanced size rows where needed. Protect the route to later streets when moving early-street probability mass. Update on rolling blocks rather than reacting hand by hand. A/B-test overlays against opponents that shift their ranges or target deterministic sizings. The operating reference is always the frozen blueprint with adaptation off.

## 5. Prioritized implementation rulebook

1. **Legality first.** Use the engine's price, open/reopen state, effective stacks, active order and pot eligibility. Treat a short all-in that fails to reopen differently from a full raise. Round in chip units, merge duplicate target sizes, and sample only legal actions.
2. **Route preflop before evaluating cards.** RFI → unopened/SB row. One raise → first-response row. Add overcall/squeeze, cold 4-bet, opener versus 3-bet, 3-bettor versus 4-bet and call-versus-jam rows. Stack-relevant all-ins trump nearest normal size. Use 169 classes with probabilities and actual chip prices.
3. **Apply a depth policy.** Use a dedicated shallow row when the effective depth changes. In SB vs BB at ≤10bb, use the Nash push/fold interval mask for a constrained fallback. Use separate multi-seat and reshove rows and evaluate exact all-in equity versus the jam range.
4. **Resolve range state and board.** Identify pot class, relative position, betting history, player count, SPR, canonical board and hand bucket. Pull range-equity and nut-mass summaries. Use the exact board row when present. Back off to texture or a conservative formula row when absent.
5. **Facing a bet, screen fold/call/raise.** Start with `C/(P_now+C)` for a closing terminal call. Before river use realization and future action estimates. Check-raise with strong value plus draws and selective backdoors. Recompute against the actual size after mapping it to a policy bin.
6. **Flop initiative.** Start with 25–33% pot when range equity supports a frequent bet. Check more on low connected or defending-range boards. Add 50–75% and an occasional overbet where the top end warrants it. Use narrower multiway betting, strong check branches and safe fallback textures.
7. **Turn and probes.** Re-evaluate relative nuts and draws on the new card. Bet for value or denial on favorable runouts, protect checks and use separate probe, delay and donk rows. Use pot geometry for stable polar stack-offs, then choose the nearest validated legal size.
8. **River.** Compute terminal price and compare bet, check and raise payoff against the action-conditioned range. Use small sizes for viable thin value and larger sizes for polar value. Fill polar bluff mass with showdown-poor blockers, preserving folds. Remove bluffs first when several defenders continue.
9. **Exploit last.** Apply only pretested overlays with matched opportunity counts, directional confidence, conservative EV and a capped probability shift. Disable on drift, model disagreement, an uncovered spot or a high-cost line with sparse data.
10. **Mix privately.** Normalize probabilities after merging rounded and illegal actions, preserve coverage by line and draw the final action once with an independent private RNG stream. Log the public node key, policy version, winning action and probability vector for replay and audit.

### Keep the decision path bounded

Use dense typed arrays for popular preflop keys, compact postflop row IDs and immutable overlays built between hands or batches. A row with up to four actions can use an action mask plus up to three byte-sized cumulative cutoffs after dropping zero-weight actions. The last cutoff is implicitly 256. Read a private uniform integer from 0 through 255 and choose the first cutoff strictly above it. Even 1 million four-byte policy rows occupy about **4 MB** before metadata and feature tables. Track and normalize memory by the number of actual reachable states. Compute cards and board descriptors incrementally and use small indexed arrays for stats. Cap the path at one feature derivation, a fixed number of table reads and fallback attempts, a handful of EV/legality comparisons and one draw. Keep sample accounting, range fitting, posterior quantiles, equilibrium solving, rollout and expensive diagnostics outside the decision path, with no synchronous wait on their results.

For off-tree bets, bracket observed sizes in the correct pot/stack units, apply a validated interpolation or randomized translation, and use the real price, stack and reopen status. Preserve enough range-state information that two different previous actions with similar sizes do not necessarily share the next policy row. If the price is far out of distribution, use a pot-odds and range-shape fallback. Pluribus used randomized pseudo-harmonic translation in some preflop branches and live search otherwise. This bot needs a tested approximation everywhere. [Pluribus supplement](https://noambrown.com/papers/19-Science-Superhuman_Supp.pdf)

### Validation for 50,000+ hands

- **Strategy integrity.** Recompute combo-weighted action totals and reach weights. Check probability sums, legality, chip conservation, exact call EV in terminal fixtures, board-suit symmetry, monotonic size sanity and off-tree edge cases. Sweep representative lines including shallow BvB, heads-up single-raised and 3-bet pots, and three-way side-pot spots.
- **Performance.** Benchmark the actual decision function on the target runtime after warmup, then include cold, mixed-spot, dirty-cache and allocation/GC pressure runs. Report p50/p95/p99/p99.9, maximum and number of decisions above **100 µs**. Budget internal p99 initially at **≤50 µs** for headroom. A percentile by itself cannot establish a hard ceiling on a general-purpose runtime. Measure a low-cost legal fallback and state the timing boundary explicitly.
- **Win rate.** Rotate seats and blind positions and use matched decks and multiple independent seeds. Run at least **50,000 hands per key lineup or paired treatment comparison**. Compare identical blueprints with overlays on/off against fixed strong bots, deliberately leaky variants and opponent bots that switch strategies mid-run. Report bb/100, block confidence intervals and the frequency and size of activated deviations. Publish both raw outcomes and any validated variance-reduced estimate.
- **Robustness.** Measure approximate best-response value in tractable two-player abstractions, plus head-to-head and six-player loss against a fixed attacker panel. Use a seed go/no-go floor such as a **≤1 bb/100 deterioration budget** against strong and adaptive controls, with uncertainty included. A finite opponent panel only estimates robustness. Test simultaneous opponents and hold out lineups/trees/seeds used to tune heuristics. Use a deadband or disable adjustment when intervals are inconclusive.
- **Interpretation.** If per-hand SD is 8bb, a simple independent-hands calculation gives a 95% interval of about **±7.0 bb/100 at 50,000 hands**. This is an illustration, with actual variance measured from the experiment. Match rotation and duplicate decks can help. AIVAT can reduce variance when the necessary explicit policies and chance model are known. Block by session or duplicated-deal group to retain dependence, and extend runs for small edges. [ACPC duplicate-match reporting](http://www.computerpokercompetition.org/index.php/competitions/rules?id=80) · [AIVAT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06915)
